## What already exists (so it is not rebuilt)

- **`app/sync`:** the `Transport` and `Link` interfaces, `GroupRoom` (an opaque room name from the group id), `InMemoryNetwork`, the `Chunker`, a WebSocket transport and probe (M0 spike code). It has its own small `PublicKey` class and no dependency on `core-model` yet.
- **Core:** `EventLog.summary()` (per author, the highest counter with no gap below it), `missingFor(summary)`, `conflictedIds()` (two different events with one id), deterministic replay, and the rule that a conflicted id has no effect but still counts as present.
- **Envelopes:** `author`, `counter`, `groupId` and `epoch` are readable without any key; everything else is encrypted. A phone can therefore store and pass on envelopes it cannot read (an epoch whose key it lacks). `GroupRepository.appendEnvelope` already checks group, size (64 KB) and signature, and stores identical bytes once.
- **`GroupSession`:** reads stored envelopes, keeps unreadable ones ("waiting for key" and "waiting for member"), takes keys from accepted rotation events, and (M4) the owner's phone changes the key after a leave when it refreshes. `refresh()` is the hook sync will call after storing new envelopes.

## Decisions made

- **S1 (7 Nov 2026): two different events with the same id: B.** The summary carries a short fingerprint of each author's events next to the counters. If two phones show the same counters but different fingerprints, they exchange that author's events, both versions end up on both phones, and every phone ignores both, as PROTOCOL.md says. Phones therefore always converge, even with a cheating member. Needs: the fingerprint defined in WIRE_FORMAT.md (how it is computed, so it is the same on every phone and cheap to update), tests with two versions of one event on different devices.
- **S2 (7 Nov 2026): who may connect and what they get.**
  - **A person removed by the owner receives only the removal event (answer to S2a).** She is not sent the events she missed before it, and nothing written after it. Consequence found while planning: a phone applies an event only when everything that event saw is present, and her phone will not have all of it, so the removal event cannot be *applied* there. Her phone therefore recognises a removal event addressed to her own key, checks its signature and that its author is a member she knows as the owner, and shows "You were removed from Goa Trip" from that, without applying it. What she already had stays as it was.
  - **Decided while planning, not asked (say if you object):** anyone who is, or ever was, a member may connect and hand over events; events from a removed or departed member after their cut-off are kept as cancelled entries (D5b). Someone who left on their own is sent nothing (D4: no copy), and a leaver's phone never asks for anything. A connection from a key that was never a member is refused before anything is sent, and learns nothing about the group.
- **S3 (7 Nov 2026): when a phone syncs: all four.** When the app opens; after each local change; every few minutes while the app is open; and in the background about every 15 minutes (or more, as Android allows) while the app is closed. M5 builds the first three and a `SyncScheduler` the background job will call, all tested on virtual time; the Android background job itself (WorkManager) needs real connections and is wired in M6. The retry delays of DESIGN.md (1, 5 and 30 minutes) apply to a peer that cannot be reached.
- **S4 (7 Nov 2026): rejected events are reported three ways.** (1) A log on the phone (kind, which peer, when; never the content). (2) A note on the Sync screen ("1 entry from Ben was rejected"). (3) A message to the person: one dismissible notice per sync run ("Some entries from Ben were rejected"), never one per envelope. A phone that sends only bad data is disconnected after a limit.
- **S5 (7 Nov 2026): the Sync screen is built in all builds.** The per-group screen of mockup 05 without the Nearby button (M6) and without the relay card (the app decides, 4 Nov): "Sync now", one line per member (how they are reached, when last, "3 entries not shared", "Not reachable directly" and so on), the rejected-entries note. On a real phone every member shows "Not reachable yet" until M6 gives it real connections. Debug builds can connect pretend devices through the fake network. The tab's "later update" text goes away.
- **S6 (7 Nov 2026): a hidden group whose leave nobody received: "Delete now" in settings.** A list of hidden groups under Settings with a **Delete now** button for each and a warning: "Nobody has seen that you left. If you delete now, the others will still count you as a member." Deleting removes the group's events and keys. A group is also deleted automatically, as in D4, once the leave has been delivered.

## Found by the simulation (T5.4), decision S7 needed

The simulation (3 to 5 phones, random operations, a faulty network, then a quiet period) fails on seed 4 and, reproducibly, on others: phones that hold the same envelopes do not agree on what happened, and a member ends up unable to read part of the log. The cause is not the sync engine. It is that the rules judge some facts against the state **at the event's place in the deterministic order**, not against **what its author had seen**. When two things happen at the same time on different phones (a member leaves while the owner is offline and rotates the key; a member is admitted while the owner removes someone), the order can put them the other way round, and an event that was valid for its author becomes invalid for everyone:

- **A key change is ignored.** The owner (offline) did not know Ben had left, so the new key was sealed to Ben, Chitra and the owner. In the order, Ben's leave comes first, the rule "sealed to exactly the current members" fails, and the event is ignored on every phone. But the owner had already written ten more entries under the new key: nobody else can open them (Chitra: "waiting for epoch 2"), and the owner's own phone still reads them with the key it saved, so the owner's view differs from everyone else's.
- **Entries are ignored.** An expense that names Ben as payer or sharer, written by someone who did not know Ben had left, is ignored (the rule: everyone named must be a current member at that point), although the rules already know how to take a departed member out of an expense.

**S7 (7 Nov 2026): A, judge by what the author saw.** Written into PROTOCOL.md section 4 ("What the author had seen") and `Replay.kt`: the members an expense or payment may name, the recipients of a key change, the target and recipients of a removal, and the epoch a leave names are all judged against the author's `after`, so an event valid for its author is valid on every phone whichever concurrent event comes first. A removal of someone who has already left still starts the new key. The owner's phone changes the key again when the key in use does not cover a leave or a member admitted at the same time (`keyChangeNeeded`, tested). Tests: `ConcurrentRulesTest` (13: each case in both orders of the events), `KeyCoverageTest` (7). **Known limit:** if the owner role changes hands at the same time as a key change by the old owner, that key change is judged by the order (the old owner may already be an admin there) and can be ignored; the sim shows whether this occurs.

## Tasks

### T5.2 Messages and wire format
Do: the sync messages as bytes, with a version number: `Hello` (group room, protocol version, my summary), `Request` (author and counter ranges I lack), `Envelopes` (a batch), `Done`/`Ack`, `Error`. Limits: a batch is at most 200 envelopes or 256 KB, an envelope at most 64 KB (the store's limit), a whole sync at most a set number of batches before it pauses and resumes. Unknown message types and newer versions are refused with a clear error, not a crash. Written into WIRE_FORMAT.md with test vectors from an independent encoder, as for the event format.
Done when: round-trip and invalid-input tests, vectors checked by the Python generator, an unknown version refused, a batch over the limits refused.


**T5.2 result (7 Nov 2026).** Specified in WIRE_FORMAT.md section 6 and built in `app/sync` (`SyncMessages.kt`): the messages `Hello` (room and summary), `Envelopes` (1 to 200 whole envelopes, at most 256 KB, each at most 64 KB), `Done` (a summary after storing), `Reconcile` (author) and `Error` (code and text), each after `'OS' | version | type`. The summary has per author the highest contiguous counter and a **fingerprint** (S1): a 16-byte chain `fp(c) = SHA-256('OTFP1' | fp(c-1) | c | n | hashes)[:16]` over the stored versions of each event (at most the two smallest by hash, so phones that swap versions end with the same two). Decoding never throws: it returns the message or a refusal with the code to tell the other side (a newer version: 1; over a size limit: 4; anything else, an unknown type included: 5). `SyncMessage.Envelopes.batches(...)` cuts a list into messages that keep every limit. The sync module now uses the core's `PublicKey` (its own copy is gone) and depends on `core-model`. **Tests:** 9 valid and 27 invalid vectors and 5 fingerprint chains from an independent Python encoder (`app/sync/src/test/tools/sync_vectors.py`), written before the Kotlin codec, all matched byte for byte; a round trip and damaged and cut input on 300 random messages (never an exception); the encoder refuses what the decoder refuses. Six rules were checked by putting the old behaviour back (fingerprint ignoring the earlier events, an unsorted summary accepted, the batch byte limit ignored, trailing bytes accepted, the version not checked, more than two versions counted): each failed tests. **Also in this work:** `main` was repaired after PR #23 (Kotlin 2.4 and AGP 8.13 on Gradle 9.8) by reverting it in PR #26 and holding those bumps back in Dependabot until the AGP 9 migration.

### T5.3 The sync engine (plain Kotlin)
Do: one sync session over one `Link`: exchange summaries, work out what each side lacks, send it in batches, verify each envelope (group, size, signature, known author) before storing it, store it only if new, answer with what was stored. Idempotent (running it twice stores nothing the second time), safe when the link drops at any point, and the engine never reads message bodies (only the fields in the clear). `sync` depends on `core-model` and `crypto`; the duplicate `PublicKey` in `app/sync` is replaced by the core's.
Done when: two stores with different histories converge; a second sync is a no-op; each of the following is tested: link closed after every possible message, a duplicate batch, a batch out of order, an envelope for another group, an oversized envelope, a bad signature (dropped, counted, the rest of the batch still stored).


**T5.3 result (7 Nov 2026).** `SyncEngine` (plain Kotlin, `app/sync`) runs one sync of one group over one `Link`: both sides send a `Hello`; the initiator sends what the responder lacks (worked out from its summary before anything arrives) and a `Done`; the responder stores it all, sends what the initiator lacks and a `Done` with its summary after storing (the proof of delivery for T5.9). Everything is stored as it arrives, so a stop loses nothing. It never opens an envelope. Results: completed, interrupted (link closed or failed), timed out (30 s of silence, on virtual time in tests), peer error, or refused (this phone ended it and told the peer with an `Error`), with counts of sent, stored, duplicate and rejected envelopes (by kind). `SyncStore` is the interface the phone's storage implements; `InMemorySyncStore` is the reference one (used by the simulation): it checks every offered envelope (`EnvelopeCheck`: size, format, group, signature), keeps at most the two smallest versions of an id and keeps the fingerprint chain up to date, also when a second version arrives later. **Tests (21):** two different histories end as the union with exactly the right send and receive counts; a second sync does nothing; an empty store receives everything; 500 events in several batches; a gap is filled; **a link cut after every possible message count (0 to the whole sync, about 20 messages) leaves nothing broken and a second sync always finishes**; a silent peer times out; a batch sent twice; events in any order; an envelope for another group, a bad signature and a malformed envelope are each dropped and counted while the rest of the batch is stored; a peer that sends only bad data is cut off after a limit with the right error; an envelope over the limit, a different room, a message out of turn and garbage each end the sync with the right error code and store nothing; a peer `Error` is reported. Mutations checked by putting the old behaviour back: the bad-envelope limit, the signature check, the group check, the fingerprint chain rebuild, and `Done` sent with the summary from before storing: each failed tests. One change (working out what to send after receiving instead of before) gives identical results in every case, so no test can tell the two apart; it is kept before, as the comment says, so what the peer just sent is plainly never sent back. **Not yet:** the exchange when fingerprints differ (T5.5), who may connect (T5.7), the log of rejected envelopes (T5.6). The spec text for `Done` in WIRE_FORMAT.md was made exact (initiator first, responder last).

### T5.5 Same id, different event (S1)
Do: the fingerprint in the summary (T5.2) and the exchange when it differs; the decision written into PROTOCOL.md. The fingerprint of an author's events is computed from the envelopes' content hashes in counter order, so it changes when any version differs and is the same on every phone.
Done when: a test builds two versions of one event on two devices that each believe they are up to date; after one sync both hold both versions, both ignore the event, and balances agree; the fingerprint is the same for the same envelopes on any phone and different for any difference.


**T5.5 result (7 Nov 2026).** The fingerprint exchange (S1: B) is in `SyncEngine`. After the `Hello`s the phone whose history of an author is at least as long as the peer's compares its own fingerprint at the peer's counter with the peer's; a difference means some event exists in different versions. The initiator sends a `Reconcile` for each author it found, then what the responder lacks plus every version it holds of those authors, then `Done`; the responder does the same for what it found and for what it was asked, and if it sent any `Reconcile` the initiator answers with the rest and a last `Done` (WIRE_FORMAT.md section 6, PROTOCOL.md section 3). Both phones end with the same two versions and the same fingerprint, so the event is ignored (`DUPLICATE_ID`) on every phone. **Engine tests (6 new, 27 in all):** same counters with different versions found and both versions exchanged, a second sync then does nothing; the longer phone notices (either side initiating) and the shorter one also gets the rest of the history; a difference only beyond the shorter phone's events is not a fork; only the author whose versions differ is reconciled; three versions across two phones end as the two smallest on both; a link cut at every message during a reconcile always recovers. `ForkTest`: three phones with real sessions, two of which each hold a different version of Ben's expense (300 and 3,000 rupees) and count different amounts; one sync later both ignore it, balances agree, and Ben's own phone agrees after catching up. **The simulation now includes a cheating phone** that hands a different version of one of its own events to another phone: 500 seeds pass with 682 such forks and 24,649 syncs. Without the fingerprint exchange 18 of 25 scenarios fail (mutation), and three more mutations of the exchange are caught.

**A hole found by the simulation and fixed.** The sync layer keeps the two versions of an id with the smallest hashes, but the database stored every version and the session read all of them. A cheater who sealed the same event again (another nonce, same content) could push the really different version out of one phone's view while another phone, which had stored all three, still saw a conflict: stored data agreed across phones, the verdicts did not (about 1.4 percent of seeds). Now `GroupRepository.envelopes` returns only the two smallest versions per id (the same two the sync keeps and compares) and `GroupSession` starts its log again from what is stored when a later arrival pushes out one it had already taken in. `ForkVariantsTest`: in 20 random worlds with three envelopes for one id (two with the same content, one different) every one of the six orders of arrival gives exactly the state of all arriving at once, with at most two envelopes read per id; both mutations (no cap, no rebuild) are caught. Seed repetition note: the nonces of the cheater's second version are random, so a failing fork seed does not repeat exactly; the failure message now compares states field by field and shows how many versions each phone stored.

### T5.6 Bad events: drop and report (S4)
Do: an envelope with a bad signature, a wrong group or too large is not stored; it is counted and recorded in a log on the phone (kind, which peer, when); an unknown author is kept for later as in M2 (it may be admitted later), a bad signature is not. The sync result carries the counts so the Sync screen (T5.10) can show the note and the one notice per run.
Done when: tests for each kind; the log never contains the envelope's content; a peer that sends only bad data is disconnected after a limit; one notice per sync run however many envelopes were rejected.


**T5.6 result (7 Nov 2026).** What the engine already did (T5.3): an envelope with a bad signature, for another group, over the size limit or not an envelope is not stored, is counted by kind in `SyncResult.rejected`, and a peer that sends more than 20 bad envelopes in one sync is cut off with `Error TOO_MANY_BAD_ENVELOPES`. New: **`SyncRecorder`** turns a sync result into (1) **lines in a log on the phone**, one per kind with its count, the peer, the group and the time (kinds: malformed, wrong group, too large, bad signature, and "cut off"), and (2) **one notice per run** (`RunNotice`: peer, total, kinds, cut off), or null when nothing was dropped; an interrupted, timed-out or peer-error sync is not a problem of envelopes and records nothing. The log (`SyncProblemLog`; in memory, and `RoomSyncProblemLog` in a new table `sync_problems`, **database version 3** with `MIGRATION_2_3`, schema `3.json`) keeps the newest 200 lines per group and goes with the group; it has **no column that could hold any part of an envelope** (only ids, a kind, a count and a time). The Sync screen (T5.10) shows the latest lines as its note and the notice once. 7 JVM tests: a clean sync records nothing; a line per kind with the right count, peer and time; one notice however many were dropped (150 in one run is one notice); a cut-off peer is logged and noticed as such; other ended syncs record nothing; the log keeps the newest lines per group, newest first, another group has its own allowance, clearing one group leaves the other; and a real engine run in which a peer sends a forged envelope twice and a junk envelope carrying a marker text: dropped, logged as 2 bad signatures and 1 malformed, and neither the log nor the notice contains the marker or any part of it. **Emulator (data, 36 tests, 0 failures):** a version 2 file made from the exported schema with a group and a join request migrates to 3, validated against `3.json`, keeps its data, gets the empty log, and the log goes with the group; the Room log keeps the newest lines per group and its table has exactly the columns row id, group, peer, kind, count and time. Five mutations (a notice when nothing was dropped, cut-off not detected, a bad signature logged as malformed, the log never pruned, one line per envelope instead of per kind) each failed tests. **Not yet:** `SyncRecorder` is called by the sync coordinator (T5.8) and shown by the Sync screen (T5.10); an unknown author is kept, not dropped, as in M2.

### T5.7 Who may connect (S2)
Do: the access rules from S2, checked before any data is sent: a link from a key that was never a member is refused; a member gets everything; a person removed by the owner is sent only the removal event; a person who left is sent nothing; events they hand over are stored and, after their cut-off, listed as cancelled. On the removed person's phone: recognise a removal event addressed to the own key (signature checked, author known as the owner) and show "You were removed" without applying it; a forged one (not from the known owner) is ignored.
Done when: tests for a member, an outsider, a removed member, a leaver and a forged removal; an outsider learns nothing about the group; the removed person's phone shows the state and keeps what it had.


**T5.7 result (7 Nov 2026).** **Engine:** `SyncEngine` takes a `PeerAccess` and asks it, by the key of the link, before it sends anything: `Refuse` (never a member: a phone that was connected to answers one `Error` code 3 and nothing else, one that connected says nothing; nothing the peer sent is read), `Full` (a member), or `Limited(summary, send)` (no longer a member: shown only the summary it may see, recomputed when sent so it shows what was just stored; sent only `send`; no fingerprints compared; its `Reconcile` not answered; what it hands over is stored). A phone decides again after it has read what the peer sent and before it sends its own part, because a leaver's first connection looks like a member's and the leave is in what they send. **Data layer:** `MembershipAccess` decides from the group's state as this phone knows it: never a member is refused; a member gets everything; someone removed by the owner gets only the removal event (written under the key they hold) and a summary clipped to what the removal had seen; someone who left gets nothing and sees only their own entry, which after the sync proves their leave arrived. A removed person's phone recognises a removal event addressed to its own key and written by the owner as it knows them, even when it cannot be applied (it depends on events it never had): `GroupView.youWereRemoved`; a removal forged by an ordinary member is not taken for it. The Groups card then says "You were removed from this group", shows no balance and the group no longer counts in the totals or the Settle tab. **Tests:** 5 engine tests (a refused peer gets only an Error and learns nothing, a refusing initiator sends nothing, a limited peer is sent only what it may have and its events are stored, a peer who may receive nothing and the answer that proves its events arrived, and a limited initiator answering a reconcile) and 6 access tests with real sessions (never a member, a member, removed by the owner with the late entry listed as cancelled, a leaver, a removal that cannot be applied, a forged removal); 1 UI test; the **simulation now runs under the access rules** (a final check among current members, and "someone no longer a member holds nothing the members lack, except another version of an event they hold"): **500 seeds pass, 24,505 syncs, 666 forks.** Mutations (the engine ignoring Refuse, a limited initiator not answering a reconcile, no second look after the peer's envelopes, a limited peer sent the full history, a removed or never-member peer treated as a member, a removal by anyone accepted) each failed tests.

**Two faults found by the simulation on the way, both fixed.** (1) **A deadlock:** a limited phone that connects to a full phone which finds a fingerprint difference (a fork handed to someone who has since left) left it waiting for a last `Done` and it timed out in every settle round (70 of 500 scenarios). A limited initiator now answers, with no envelopes. (2) **A loop in M4's key duty:** `refresh()` calls the owner's key change, which ended by calling `refresh()` again; in one scenario in 500 it recursed until the stack overflowed (with the guard removed seed 469 loops again). A key change no longer re-runs the duty from inside itself, a refused one is not retried until something new has arrived, and the simulation now checks that after everything settles no owner still wants to change the key and none changed it more than 25 times (the most seen: 3). The root cause of the one transient state is not known; the guard makes the loop impossible and the invariant would show a repeat. **Emulator (data, 36 tests):** pass. **Note:** the access check refreshes the session at each connection (a replay of the whole log); a 500-seed run takes about 8 minutes now (3 to 4 before), which T5.8 must keep in mind for large groups (T5.11).

### T5.8 Sync in the data layer
Do: `SyncCoordinator` on top of `Sessions`: for a group, connect to the other members, run the engine, call `GroupSession.refresh()` after new envelopes arrive (so the owner's phone changes the key after a leave, T4.11), and publish a status (idle, syncing with N, waiting for a friend, last result per member, rejected counts). Triggers (S3): when the app opens, after each local change, every few minutes while open (all on virtual time in tests), retries after 1, 5 and 30 minutes for a peer that cannot be reached, and a `SyncScheduler` that the Android background job calls in M6.
Done when: two real `Sessions` on two in-memory stores, joined to one fake network, converge after a change on either side; a leave written on one phone makes the owner's phone change the key by itself on the other.


**T5.8 result (7 Nov 2026).** **`SyncCoordinator`** (data layer, on top of `Sessions` and the sync engine): `start(group)` answers every member who connects (the engine as responder, with `MembershipAccess`), syncs at once, again after each change this phone's user makes (a rise in `GroupView.ownCounter`, new), and every 5 minutes while the group is open (S3); `stop(group)`; `syncNow(group)` ("Sync now": every member, ignoring waiting times, returns when all were tried); `runOnce()` (one pass over every readable group and then done: the function the Android background job of M6 will call); `dismissNotice(group)`. **Retries:** a member who cannot be reached, or a sync that did not finish, is tried again after 1 minute, then 5, then 30, then every 30 (DESIGN.md), and the periodic pass leaves such a member alone until its time; success forgets the failures. **One sync per member at a time:** a change made while a sync is under way is sent by one more sync straight after, not lost and not one sync per keystroke.
