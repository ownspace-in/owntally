# Security review (prepared for the owner's human review)

M10, T10.8. **BUILD_PLAN rule 8 needs a human to review the security-relevant files; this document prepares that review and does not replace it.** It is written by the builder (Claude) after reading the files below in full or in the parts named, running mutation tests against the guards, and probing one suspicion with a test. **The gate stays open until the owner (or a person they choose) has read the files and signed it off at the end of this document.**

Date of the review: 10 Nov 2026, commit after `71391b6`. Reviewed by the builder only, by reading and by mutation testing: no external tool, no penetration test, no fuzzing, no review of the Rust code of Iroh or of the relay.

## 1. Findings (read these first)

None is a way to read a group's data without its key, or to forge another member's entry. Four were real weaknesses against a **malicious member** (or a former member): F1, F3 and F4 are now fixed with the owner's approval, F2 is a known limit for 1.0 by the owner's decision; I also fixed two small things (F5, F6); three are notes.

| # | Severity | Finding | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Medium to high | **One member can make key changes impossible, including their own removal.** A member's encryption key is whatever 32 bytes the joiner's request holds (`JoinRequestBody.decode` only checks the size). If it is a low-order X25519 point (all zeros, 1, or the other small-order encodings), `KeyRotation.seal` throws `InvalidKeyException` for everyone: the owner's "Remove" and "Change key" then fail while that member is in the group, and `GroupSession.rotating` lets the exception out. Probed and now recorded by `KeyRotationUnusableKeyTest` (all three encodings throw). Replay cannot skip such a member because a rotation must seal to exactly the members the author knew (`sealedKeys.keys == knownTo(event)`). Needs an admin to approve the request, so the attacker is someone the group let in. | **Fixed (owner approved the rule, 10 Nov 2026).** Replay ignores a `MemberJoined` or `GroupCreated` whose encryption key is a low-order point (`LowOrderKeys`, the seven X25519 encodings; PROTOCOL.md updated), the host refuses such a request at the door (`JoinRequestBody.decode`), and `rotating` returns `AddResult.Invalid` instead of throwing. Tests: `LowOrderKeysTest` (the list, both replay rules), `JoinRequestLowOrderKeyTest`, and `KeyRotationUnusableKeyTest` (every encoding in the list is one HPKE refuses). Mutations (list's top bit, each of the three checks removed) all caught. The `rotating` handler has no test of its own: it cannot be reached once replay refuses such members. |
| F2 | Medium | **Any validly signed envelope from any key is stored and passed on.** `EnvelopeCheck`/`GroupRepository.appendEnvelope` check the group and the signature against the envelope's own author, not that the author is a member (the author check is made later, when opening). A current or former member can push any number of envelopes signed by throwaway keys (each up to 64 KB): they are stored on every phone that syncs with them and relayed onward, so storage grows on all phones and nothing removes it. Strangers cannot do this: a peer that was never a member is refused before anything is read (`Admission.Refuse`). | **Known limit for 1.0 (owner decision, 10 Nov 2026).** Proposal kept for an update: a cap per author and per group. |
| F3 | Medium to low | **A peer's messages queue without limit.** `SyncEngine`, `JoinHost`, `JoinClient` and `SocketLink` copy what a link delivers into `Channel.UNLIMITED` while the engine works through it (each envelope is a database write). A member peer that sends faster than the phone stores grows the phone's memory; frames are up to 64 KB (relay) or 1 MB (local). The 30-second stall timeout does not help against a peer that keeps sending. | **Fixed (owner approved, 10 Nov 2026).** The queues between a link and its reader hold at most `LinkQueue.CAPACITY` (64) messages where the reader can wait (the sync engine, the join host and client, the first-message hand-over in `SyncCoordinator`, a socket: the sender then waits, by TCP), and `CALLBACK_CAPACITY` (256, frames of at most 64 KB) where a library calls back and cannot wait (the relay's WebSocket link, the relay stream, Nearby): there a full queue ends the link. `InboundQueueTest` floods the engine while the store is stuck: 22 million messages queued in one second before, at most 64 now. The queues of *new connections* (`Channel<Link>`) are still unbounded and were not changed. A legitimate very large burst over a callback transport that exceeds 256 frames before the phone catches up would now end the link; the next sync carries on from what was stored. |
| F4 | Low | **Member names can imitate each other and can contain direction-changing characters.** The owner decided (4 Nov) that look-alike letters are not "the same name". `NameKey` strips invisible characters only to compare names; the name shown keeps them, so a name with a right-to-left override can display reversed. In an app that moves money between friends, a member named like another is a social-engineering path. | **Fixed (owner decision, 10 Nov 2026: any language, no invisible or direction characters, names only).** `NameText` in the core, applied by `checkName` to the group's name and members' names (not to expense descriptions); ZWNJ/ZWJ stay allowed between letters for Persian, Malayalam, Kannada and similar scripts; the four screens that check names (first run, Create group, Approve, Profile) use the same rule and say why. PROTOCOL.md updated. Tests: `NameTextTest` and one in each screen's model test; mutations (direction characters allowed, joiner allowed anywhere, rule not applied) caught. |
| F7 | Low to medium | **A stranger can block a phone's inbound connections over Iroh.** `IrohPortImpl.incoming` accepts one connection at a time and waits in `conn.acceptBi()` for it to open its stream, with no time limit of its own. Anyone who knows a member's key (it is the Iroh endpoint id and is published to n0's discovery) can connect and say nothing: until the connection times out, every other inbound connection waits behind it, and repeating it keeps the phone from being reached. Outgoing syncs are not affected. Not testable on the JVM (native library). | **Fixed (owner approved, 10 Nov 2026).** `IrohPort.incoming()` now hands over a connection that is *accepted but not yet opened* (`IncomingIroh`); `IrohTransport` opens each in its own coroutine with `acceptWindowMs` (10 s) and allows `maxPendingAccepts` (16) at once, dropping the rest. Tests with a fake port: a silent stranger does not hold up a later connection, is dropped after the window, and more than the limit are dropped at once; removing each of the three guards is caught. **Not tested:** the real `IrohPortImpl.Pending` (the native library cannot run on the JVM): the smoke test of the shipped APK starts Iroh and passes, but no real incoming connection was made. Two real phones remain the check, and whether cancelling a native `accept().connect()` on timeout releases it cleanly is unproven. |
| F8 | Low | **Resources a stranger can use up by knowing a room or a member key.** (a) `WebSocketTransport.dispatch` makes a `RelayLink` and queues it for every new sender key in a frame; the relay has no client authentication (N3), so the sender is whatever the sender says, and the table and the unbounded `Channel<Link>` of new connections (also in Iroh, Nearby, local links) grow with each new key. `SecureOverCarrier` checks the TLS certificate against the named sender, so no one is *accepted* as another member, but links still pile up. (c) `PlayNearbyPort`'s event queue (`Channel.UNLIMITED`, fed by Google's callbacks) can grow under a flood of payloads from someone in Bluetooth range; payloads are at most 32 KB. (b) `SyncCoordinator.respond` is started for every incoming link without a limit, and for a non-join message it first builds a `RepositorySyncStore` (reads every envelope of the group from the database) before the engine decides the peer is not a member. The room needs the group id, so (a) is for people who have seen an invitation or are members. | **Fixed (owner asked for it, 11 Nov 2026, after deciding on a later update).** (1) `SyncCoordinator.respond`: a connection from a key the phone does not know waits for its first message in a **small pool of its own** (16) for at most 10 seconds, so strangers cannot take the places of members (a known member has a pool of 64); a **stranger's sync is refused before anything is read from the database** (the phone looks at the group again for an unknown peer at most once in 5 seconds, to see whether they were just admitted); join conversations (an invitation holder who is not a member) share a pool of 8; syncs with members a pool of 16 per group. (2) The queues of new connections are bounded (`LinkQueue.INCOMING`, 64) in Iroh and the relay's WebSocket transport, and a full queue closes the new connection instead of keeping it; (3) a relay room keeps links for at most 64 different senders. Tests (`StrangerLimitsTest`, 3; `IrohTransportTest` +1; `WebSocketTransportTest` +1), six mutations caught: strangers' syncs not refused, strangers sharing the members' pool, no join limit, no limit on looking at the group, the Iroh queue unbounded again, links not capped. A test of mine found a flaw in the first design (strangers who stayed silent filled one shared pool and a member was turned away), which is why the pools are separate. **Not done:** the queues of new connections in the local-network link, `SecureOverCarrier` and Nearby, and Nearby's event queue, stay unbounded (they need a phone near the one attacked, or an already-admitted key). A determined flood of connections from many keys can still delay *inbound* syncs for the 10 seconds each silent connection waits; the phone's own outgoing syncs are not affected. |

| N1 | Note | **The member's signing seed is also Iroh's secret key** (decision I2): one Ed25519 key is used by two protocols. Signatures made by the app all start with their own label (`owntally/v1/...`) and Iroh's start with theirs, so a signature for one cannot be taken for the other, but a flaw in Iroh's use of the key would touch the member's key. Accepted by the owner in I2; listed for the human reviewer. | Accepted. |
| N2 | Note | **Open invitations survive a key change.** `Invites.revoke` is manual; nothing revokes the open tickets when a member is removed or the key changes. A ticket carries the key of the epoch it was made in, so someone who joins with it can read that epoch (and any admin approval is still needed). Low, because the link is a secret that expires in 8 hours. | **Fixed before 1.0 (owner decision, 10 Nov 2026).** An invitation works only while the key it carries is the group's current key (`Invites.isForCurrentKey`, used by the join host and by the lists on the screen), so a key change or a removal cancels every open invitation on every admin's phone as soon as that phone learns of it. A joiner with an old link gets the same refusal as for an expired one. Tests: `InvitesTest` (key change, removal); mutations (host check removed, list not filtered) caught. |
| N4 | Note | **Nearby links are authenticated once, at the start.** The Nearby handshake (`LinkAuth` proofs over both nonces and both keys) proves who is at each end, but a Nearby link is `PLAIN`: later messages carry no authentication of their own and rely on Nearby's own encrypted channel. `PlayNearbyPort.acceptConnection` accepts every connection without comparing Nearby's pairing digits, so Nearby's own channel is not authenticated either: a person between two phones could pass the signed handshake along and then change later control messages, for example a closing summary that would pass for the proof that a leave arrived (D4), so a phone that left could delete its copy before the leave arrived. Over the relay and Iroh this is avoided with TLS or QUIC. Envelopes are signed and encrypted in any case. | Accepted in M6; listed for the reviewer. |
| N3 | Note | **The relay has no client authentication** (M9 finding, carried since). Read in the third pass (`relay/ws-relay/main.go`, `hub.go`, the nginx file): the relay stamps each forwarded frame with the sender id the *sender* gave in its address (`?id=`), and a new connection with the same id **replaces** the old one. So anyone who knows a room and a member's public key can (a) knock that member off the relay, again each time they reconnect (limited to 300 connects a minute per address in the deployed service), (b) flood that member until the relay drops them as "too slow" (50 frames a second, queue of 64), and (c) send frames that say they come from that member. (c) cannot make anyone a member: the app checks the sender against the TLS certificate (`SecureOverCarrier`). (a) and (b) are denial of service on the relay path only; Iroh and the local network are unaffected. What the relay sees is as the privacy text says. nginx overwrites `X-Forwarded-For`, so the per-address limit cannot be faked. | **Fixed in M12 (owner decisions D1 and D2, 11 Nov 2026).** The relay admits a connection for a key only to the holder of that key: it sends a fresh nonce, the client signs a label, the room, its key and the nonce, and the relay sends ready or closes with 4401 (not the holder) or 4408 (no answer in 5 seconds); until ready the client is in no room, so nobody can replace a member's connection, flood them or speak as them on the relay path. Small-order keys are refused; there is no optional mode. Tests: the Go relay (12 new, with the app's independent vectors), `RelayAuthTest`, `WebSocketTransportTest` (+6), and `RealRelayTest` against the real binary (an impostor naming a member's key is refused and the member stays connected). Mutations: 7 in the relay and 4 in the app, all caught. See docs/tasks/M12-relay-signin.md. **Still true:** the relay sees public keys, the room, addresses, timing and sizes; it cannot be made to forget them. A holder of a member's key is that member. |

Not found: a nonce reuse (Tink makes a fresh 24-byte nonce for every call), a signature that can be moved between groups, epochs, authors, counters or purposes, a secret in a log or `toString` (`NoLoggingTest`, `KeySecrecyGuardTest`), a network call other than sync (`NetworkUseGuardTest`), a key that reaches the backup (the extraction rules take the database only; I did not check on a device what Android actually backs up), or a component exported besides the launcher activity.

**Findings from reading the M11 code (none blocks release; the first two are inherent in the design the owner chose):**

- **R1 (note; text added 11 Nov 2026 at the owner's request). A leaked phrase is now an open door to every group, with no approval.** *Now said plainly on the phrase screen and in the privacy text (`PHRASE_WARNING`, `RECOVERY_LEAK`; `PromisesTest` pins both).* Keys come from the phrase and the group id, so anyone with the 12 words who also has any invitation to a group is recognised by the host as that member and joins without an admin: before M11 they could derive the keys but would have needed an approval to be let in. DESIGN.md already says anyone with the phrase can act as the person in every group. There is no remedy short of a **new phrase** (a new identity): leaving and coming back moves to the next generation, but the next generation's key is derived from the same phrase, so the attacker has it too. The app does not say this in one sentence anywhere yet; the recovery-phrase screen's warning ("Anyone with them can act as you in every group") is the nearest.
- **R2 (note). Key chaining means one epoch's key opens every earlier epoch.** Anyone who holds the newest key can read all earlier history. Every phone that holds the newest key already received the history by sync (the design), and a removed member never gets the newest key, so nothing a member could not read is opened; what is lost is that an outsider who steals only the newest key from one phone also gets the past (that phone held the past keys too).
- **R3 (low, a form of F2). A member can write many `KeyLinked` events** with wrong keys. Adoption tries each against the entries waiting for that epoch, so its cost grows with the number of links times the number of waiting entries. No wrong key can be stored (authenticated decryption); the cost is only time, on a late member's phone, and the members who could do it are those F2 already covers.
- **R4 (note). A restored member does not use up the invitation.** The host answers a status question without consuming the ticket, so the same link (valid up to 8 hours, until the key changes) can still be used by someone else with a request an admin approves.
